◈ About This Panchangam

AMANTA SYSTEM (ಅಮಾಂತ)

This Panchangam follows the Amanta (also called Amavasyant) lunar calendar system, which is traditional to Karnataka, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, and Gujarat. In the Amanta system, the lunar month ends on Amavasya (New Moon). The month begins on Pratipada of Shukla Paksha the day after Amavasya.

PURNIMANTA vs AMANTA

The Purnimanta system (used in North India — UP, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh) ends the month on Purnima (Full Moon). This means the Krishna Paksha in Purnimanta belongs to the current month's name, while in Amanta the same Krishna Paksha belongs to the next month's name. For example: Kartika Krishna Ekadashi in Purnimanta = Ashwina Krishna Ekadashi in Amanta.

KANNADA PANCHANGAM TRADITION

The Kannada Panchangam is published annually by institutions such as the Sri Rambhapuri Peetham, Hubli and Uttaradi Math, Bengaluru. The five elements of Panchang are: Tithi, Vara, Nakshatra, Yoga, and Karana.

Two computation schools coexist in modern publications. This app supports both, switchable from the toggle in the header:

DRIK GANITA (DEFAULT)

Drik Ganita ("computation by sight") uses modern astronomical theory — VSOP87 planetary motion and ELP-2000 lunar theory — to compute the true geocentric longitudes of the Sun and Moon at any moment. Tithi boundaries are the moments when their elongation (moon − sun) crosses a multiple of 12°. The tropical-to-sidereal conversion uses the Lahiri (Chitra paksha) ayanamsha. Matches contemporary publications such as drikpanchang.com to within ~1 arcminute.

TRADITIONAL KARNATAKA (SURYA SIDDHANTA)

The Surya Siddhanta is the classical Indian astronomical treatise (final form ~12th century CE), and is the basis of the Saurapaksha computation followed by Karnataka and Madhwa-tradition publications. It uses yuga-based mean motions and a single-epicycle equation of centre (mandaphala) — far simpler than modern lunar theory, and consequently can disagree with Drik Ganita by 0–7° in moon longitude on any given day. Because elongation advances at only ~12°/day, this translates to tithi-start differences of up to several hours, which is the well-known and accepted Drik-vs-Saurapaksha gap.

This implementation uses the canonical Burgess (1860) yuga constants with zero bija correction. Different Madhwa/Karnataka schools historically apply different bija (time-linear) corrections from their own epochs, and matching any specific publication exactly requires that publication's bija — which differs between schools. The BIJA_* constants in the code are exposed for properly-sourced calibration but ship at zero so the engine reproduces pure Surya Siddhanta rather than guessing a school's specific tuning.

For an authoritative muhurta, consult the printed panchanga of your tradition or a qualified Jyotishi — exact tithi-start times legitimately vary between publications even within the same school.

MUHURTA ZONES

Muhurtas are 48-minute time units (1/30th of a day). Special named muhurtas — Brahma Muhurta, Rahu Kaal, Gulika Kaal, Yamaganda, Abhijit — are calculated relative to local sunrise in Bengaluru (approx. 6:02–6:22 AM IST throughout the year). The Rahu Kaal shifts each day of the week. This app displays today's Muhurta zones as coloured arcs on the 24-hour dial.

DISCLAIMER

Tithi start/end times are indicative based on standard astronomical approximations for Bengaluru. For precise muhurtas for religious ceremonies (weddings, griha pravesh, upanayana), always consult a qualified Jyotishi or the current year's printed Kannada Panchangam.
